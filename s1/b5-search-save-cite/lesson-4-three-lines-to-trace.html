三行来源卡，与一眼三看The three-line source card, and the three glances

纸上的书印出来就定了型，网页却随时在动：改版、搬家、悄悄删掉。你的教师在课程读本第六讲里写过线上百科全书的妙处——"检索方便，不占空间……随时生长，实时更新"。会生长是它的本事，也是你的麻烦：今天看到的页面，三个月后可能换了模样，甚至不在了。所以本周的最后一门手艺，是给每个用到的来源留一张"卡"——三行，就够任何人（包括未来的你）沿着它追回去。

A printed book sets like plaster; a web page never stops moving — redesigned, relocated, quietly deleted. In chapter six of the course reader your instructor wrote of the online encyclopedia's charms: "easy to search, taking no shelf space… growing and updating in real time." That growth is its talent and your trouble: the page you saw today may look different in three months, or be gone. Hence the week's final craft: leave every source you use a "card" — three lines, enough for anyone (future-you included) to trace it back.

三行来源卡The three-line source card

给一个来源留档，三行足矣：① 标题 ② 作者或机构 ③ 网址 + 访问日期。这是习惯，不是论文格式——真正的学术引用格式（各种括号和斜体）以后有的是时间学；三行卡要练的是那个更根本的动作：凡用必留痕，痕迹能追回。第 10 周的立场短文、第二学期的资源评鉴报告，用的都是它。一张填好的卡长这样：Three lines archive a source completely: ① title ② author or institution ③ URL + access date. This is a habit, not academic formatting — the true citation styles, with their brackets and italics, can wait their turn; what the card trains is the more fundamental move: whatever you use leaves a trace, and the trace leads back. 一眼三看：第一眼值不值得信The three glances: does it earn first-glance trust?

  1. 谁在说？机构（疾控中心、大学、报社编辑部）背后有一群要负责的人；个人可能很专业，也可能只是转发。往页顶页脚看署名和主办方——什么都查不到的"未署名"，本身就是一个信号。Who says it? Behind an institution (a CDC, a university, a newsroom) stands a crowd of accountable people; an individual may be expert — or merely reposting. Check the page's head and foot for bylines and operators — an untraceable "unsigned" is itself a signal.
  2. 什么时候说的？找日期。没有日期的"最新研究"，可能已经十岁了。会过时的话题（健康、软件、数据），日期几乎和内容一样重要。When was it said? Find the date. An undated "latest study" may be ten years old. On topics that age — health, software, statistics — the date matters nearly as much as the content.
  3. 它引了谁？可靠的页面会交代自己的依据："据某某研究""来源：某机构"，或者页尾一排参考文献——顺着能追到原始出处。什么都不引、只说"专家表示"的页面，第一眼要打个问号。What does it cite? A reliable page shows its ground: "according to this study", "source: this institution", references at the foot — threads that lead to the original. A page citing nothing but "experts say" earns a first-glance question mark.

第三看正是维基百科这类线上百科"人人可编辑却大体可靠"的秘密——读本第六讲讲过：想编辑内容的人"必须给出权威的引用文献或资料"，而且一篇文章有来自世界各地成百上千双眼睛在读、在查、在改，"越是被更多人阅读的文章，其精准度也就越高"。所以读本给的建议是：线上百科是个不错的起点，但最好同时查阅更多专业来源——起点，不是终点。The third glance is exactly the secret of why an anyone-can-edit encyclopedia stays broadly reliable — reader chapter six explains: whoever edits "must supply authoritative citations", and each article is read, checked and corrected by hundreds of eyes worldwide — "the more an article is read, the more accurate it becomes." Hence the reader's advice: the online encyclopedia makes a fine starting point — best paired with more specialised sources; a start, never a finish.

但"很多人都这么说"从来不等于"对"。读本第六讲还讲了另一半：英国政府请网民给一艘科考船投票起名，几十万张票把冠军投给了恶搞名字"Boaty McBoatface"（差不多是中文里的"一颗赛艇"）——票数第一，仍然不是好名字。流传广、点赞多、投票高，都不在三看之内。网上信息这么多样，读本说，要做"过河的小马"——老牛说水浅、松鼠说水深，最后下水试深浅的判断，得是你自己的。Yet "everyone says so" has never meant "true". The chapter tells the other half too: the British government let the public vote on a research ship's name, and hundreds of thousands of votes crowned the joke entry "Boaty McBoatface" — first by ballots, still no name for a ship. Wide circulation, likes and votes appear nowhere in the three glances. Amid the internet's roaring variety, the reader says, be the foal at the river — the ox calls the water shallow, the squirrel calls it deep, and the judgment that finally wades in must be your own.

一眼三看管的是"第一眼"。真正的查证功夫——横向阅读、把一条说法追到源头、识别以假乱真的图文——第二学期"信息与媒介可信度"有一整个单元来练。本周先把三行卡写熟：会记录来源的人，才谈得上核查来源。The three glances govern the first glance only. The real verification craft — lateral reading, tracing a claim to its origin, spotting fabricated media — gets a whole Semester 2 module, Information & Media Credibility. This week, make the three-line card second nature: only someone who records sources can ever verify them.
