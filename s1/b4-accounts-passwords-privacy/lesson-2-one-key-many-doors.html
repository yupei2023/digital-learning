一把钥匙，别开所有的门One key must not open every door

先说一个可能让你意外的结论：多数人账号出事，不是因为密码太弱，而是因为同一个密码到处用。想象你家、教室储物柜、自行车锁用的是同一把钥匙——配钥匙的师傅、捡到钥匙的路人，任何一个环节失守，所有的门一起失守。密码也是一样：某个小网站被偷了数据（这事你拦不住，也不怪你），偷来的"邮箱 + 密码"就会被程序拿去挨个试遍所有大网站——这叫"撞库"。撞开的从来不是最结实的门，而是共用钥匙的门。

Start with a conclusion that may surprise you: when accounts fall, it is usually not because the password was weak, but because the same password was used everywhere. Imagine one key fitting your home, your school locker and your bike lock — the locksmith, the stranger who finds it, any single failure opens every door at once. Passwords work the same way: some small website gets its data stolen (which you could not prevent and is not your fault), and programs take the stolen email-plus-password pairs and try them against every major site in turn — the practice called credential stuffing. The door that gives way is never the sturdiest one; it is the one sharing a key.

看完记住三件事Three things to keep from the clip

  1. 泄露常常不怪你。网站被偷是网站的事故；你无法阻止它发生。你能决定的只有一件：丢一把钥匙时，是丢一扇门，还是丢全部的门。重要账号，一号一钥。The leak is usually not your doing. A breached website is the website's accident; you cannot prevent it. What you do decide is a single thing: when one key is lost, whether one door goes or all of them. Important accounts get one key each.
  2. 邮箱排第一。你的邮箱是所有账号的"总后台"——别的密码忘了，靠它找回；别的账号换密码，确认信发到它那里。谁掌握了你的邮箱，谁就能重置你其余的一切。所以本周任务里，邮箱的新密码必须独一无二，其余账号依次排后。Email ranks first. Your inbox is the master office behind every account — forgotten passwords are recovered through it, password-change confirmations arrive in it. Whoever holds your email can reset everything else you own. Hence in this week's task, the email account's new password must be one of a kind, with every other account queued behind it.
  3. 听到泄露消息怎么办？某平台出事上了新闻，或你收到"异地登录"提醒：先改那个账号的密码，再改所有和它共用过密码的账号——按重要程度，邮箱最先。改完就好，不必恐慌：这套动作本周练完，你终身都会。And when leak news arrives? A platform's breach makes the news, or a strange-location sign-in alert lands: change that account's password first, then every account that ever shared the password — in order of importance, email first. Once changed, the matter is closed; no panic required. Practise the sequence this week and you own it for life.

永不外传清单The never-share list

有些信息，无论对方是谁、理由多动听，都不给。这份清单本周起长期有效，讲义《账号安全卡》里有可打印的一份：Some information is given to no one, whoever they claim to be and however moving the reason. This list takes effect this week and never expires; the handout Account security card carries a printable copy:

  • 密码——包括最好的朋友、包括"帮你交作业"的同学。真有需要共用的东西，用别的办法（比如把文件发过去），钥匙不外借。Passwords — best friends included, the classmate "submitting homework for you" included. Whatever genuinely needs sharing can travel another way (send the file itself); keys are not lent.
  • 验证码——短信里那六位数字，等于一把"限时万能钥匙"。记死一条：真客服永远不会向你要验证码；来要的，无论自称是谁，都是骗局。Verification codes — the six digits in the text message are a time-limited master key. Memorise one rule: no genuine customer service ever asks for your code; whoever asks, whatever they call themselves, it is a scam.
  • 身份证号、家庭住址、学校全名、正脸照片——课程隐私四条早已包含后两样；前两样同理。ID numbers, home address, full school name, face photos — the course's four privacy rules already cover the latter two; the former two follow the same logic.
  • 别人的个人信息——你没有权利替别人公开任何东西（学习礼仪第 3 条的原话）。Other people's personal information — you hold no right to publish anything on another's behalf (Learning Etiquette rule 3, verbatim).
