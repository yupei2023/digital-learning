利与弊：它对你的大脑做了什么？Benefits and risks: what does it do to your brain?

优势：硬件、软件与丰富的笼子Benefits: hardware, software and the rich cage

读本第 05 章把大脑比作电脑的硬件，把心智——你积累的经验、知识、技能和记忆——比作软件。学习，就是给硬件不断升级软件。和电脑不同的是，人的硬件也会被软件改造：你学的东西，会真的改变你的大脑。（这个比喻有一处不准：电脑的硬件不会因为装了新软件而长出新的线路，大脑会——这正是第 05 章想说的“可塑性”。）

Ch. 05 compares the brain to a computer's hardware and the mind — your accumulated experience, knowledge, skills and memories — to software. Learning is upgrading the software on the hardware. Unlike a computer, though, your hardware is reshaped by the software: what you learn physically changes your brain. (The metaphor breaks here: a computer does not grow new circuits when you install software; a brain does — which is exactly the “plasticity” Ch. 05 is about.)

然后是小白鼠实验：住在每天重新布置、设施多样的环境里的小白鼠，比住在空盒子里的小白鼠学得更快、错得更少，视觉中枢的神经突触明显更多。结论：环境越丰富，人越聪明。数字化学习的第一项优势就在这里——同一个牛顿定律，你可以看本国老师讲、外国老师讲、动画讲、实验讲；一个问题，可以用中文问、用英文问。你在给自己的大脑布置一个更丰富的笼子。

Then the mouse experiment: mice raised in a varied environment rearranged daily learned faster and made fewer errors than mice in an empty box, with noticeably more synapses in the visual cortex. Conclusion: the richer the environment, the smarter the animal. Digital learning's first benefit lives here — the same Newton's law taught by your teacher, by a teacher abroad, by an animation, by an experiment; one question asked in Chinese and in English. You are furnishing your own brain a richer cage.

第 05 章还说了第二项优势：大脑记住的是你对经历的态度。通过网络，你可以靠近更多乐观、积极的学习者，“多与榜样在一起，模仿他们，成为他们”。

Ch. 05 adds a second benefit: what the brain keeps is your attitude toward an experience. Through the network you can get close to more optimistic, active learners — “stay with the role models, imitate them, become them”.

分心与多任务Distraction and multitasking查一个词，二十分钟后还在看短视频；一边背单词一边回消息——M1 微课 2 说过，大脑在两件事之间来回切换，两件都做不好。Look up one word, still watching short videos twenty minutes later; memorising vocabulary while replying to messages — M1's micro-lesson 2: the brain keeps switching, and both suffer.学习的 45 分钟里，手机放在另一个房间，通知全关；查资料用电脑的一个固定窗口。For the 45-minute study block, phone in another room, all notifications off; look things up in one fixed browser window on the computer.
能力错觉The illusion of competence看完一段讲得很清楚的视频，觉得“懂了”；合上视频自己讲一遍，讲不出来。视频越流畅，错觉越大。Watch a clear video and feel “I get it”; close it and try to explain — nothing comes. The smoother the video, the bigger the illusion.每看完一段视频，关掉它，用三句话把内容讲给自己听（或写在日志里），讲不出来就回去再看一遍。After every video, close it and explain the content to yourself in three sentences (or in your log); if you can't, watch again.
来源不可靠Unreliable sources搜索结果第一条不一定对；AI 会一本正经地说错话（课程的 AI 使用规则第 2 条，见第 1 周页面）。The first search result is not necessarily right; AI can be confidently wrong (course AI rule 2, on the Week 1 page).凡是要写进作业的事实，找第二个来源核对一次，并记下网址和日期（第 6 周 B5 会详细教）。Any fact going into your work gets checked against a second source, and you note its address and date (Week 6, B5, will teach this in detail).
把工具当拐杖The tool as a crutch翻译软件替你读、搜索替你想、AI 替你写——大脑没有开工，学习就没有发生（M1 第 02 章的推论——写 AI 的同学可以引这一条）。The translator reads for you, search thinks for you, AI writes for you — the brain never starts work, so no learning happens (a corollary of M1 Ch. 02 — those writing about AI may cite it).用翻译工具读英文时，关键句子自己先猜一遍再看译文；用 AI 时按教纲写清它帮了什么，观点和例子自己出。With a translator, guess each key sentence yourself before reading the translation; with AI, state in your use note what it helped with — ideas and examples are your own.

注意最右一列的写法：对策是一个动作，有时间、有地点、有对象——不是“少玩手机”“多自律”这种口号。量规第 ③ 行只认动作。

Note how the right-hand column is written: a mitigation is an action with a time, a place, an object — not a slogan like “use my phone less” or “be more disciplined”. Rubric row ③ only credits actions.

为什么这不是一场“工具好不好”的辩论Why this is not a debate about whether the tool is good

火箭和泥路是同一片土地。第 05 章说“数字化学习让学习变得更简单了，也变得更难了”——对想学的人如虎添翼，对不想学的人拉大差距。所以“学校该不该允许”这个问题，真正的分歧不在工具，而在谁在用、怎么用、在什么条件下用。这就是你的公开信要回答的。泥路尽头的女孩需要那部手机，教室里的三十部手机又在偷走注意力——你的信要同时对这两件事负责。

Rocket and mud road share the same ground. Ch. 05 says digital learning “makes learning easier — and harder”: wings for those who want to learn, a widening gap for those who don't. So in “should schools allow it?”, the real disagreement is not about the tool but about who uses it, how, and under what conditions. That is what your open letter has to answer. The girl at the end of the mud road needs that phone, and the thirty phones in a classroom are stealing attention — your letter has to answer for both.
