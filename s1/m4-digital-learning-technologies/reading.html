导语Lead-in 必读 · 约 1 分钟Required · ~1 min

电影《2001 太空漫游》中有一句话：“人猿部落并没有意识到：千千万万吨多肉多汁、徜徉在稀树草原和灌木林里的动物，不只非他们能力所及，也非他们想象所及。他们身处丰饶之中，却逐渐饥饿至死。”身处富饶，却饥饿至死？别以为这只是出现在原始社会的吊诡现象，注意观察一下你周边的世界，处处皆有。今天，我们就拿读书学习这件事来说一说。

There is a line in the film 2001: A Space Odyssey: the tribe of man-apes never realised that the thousands of tons of juicy meat roaming the savannah and the scrub were beyond not only their reach but their imagination — “in the midst of plenty, they were slowly starving to death.” Starving in the midst of plenty? Don't imagine this paradox belongs only to primitive societies; look around you and it is everywhere. Today let us take reading and learning as our example.

……在这个书籍买卖越来越便利、优质阅读资料唾手可得、网络资讯爆发式发展的现代社会，人们的读书量也随之提升了吗？从一份中国民众阅读习惯调查的数据可见，从 2011 年到 2022 年 4 月，人们年均阅读的纸质书籍和电子书籍加起来，一直都没有超过 9 本[3]。一边是与日俱增的购书量，一边是止步不前的读书量：前者代表了人们对于学习的美好愿望，后者则呈现出我们疲软的学习能力的现实。

… In a society where buying books grows ever easier, good reading material is at everyone's fingertips and online information explodes, has the amount people read risen with it? A survey of Chinese reading habits shows that from 2011 to April 2022, the average number of books read per person per year — paper and electronic together — never exceeded nine[3]. On one side, purchases climbing year by year; on the other, reading that never moves: the first stands for our fine wish to learn, the second for the feebleness of our actual capacity to learn.

藏书不等于读书Owning books is not reading them 必读 · 约 3 分钟Required · ~3 min

在我的小学时代，书籍像今天这样丰富、廉价、易得，是无法想象的。小学时的暑假，我常常跟随母亲上下班，她在北京海淀医院工作。上世纪 90 年代，海淀医院的旧址就在现在的海淀图书城，曾经那里只有一家新华书店，而后陆续开了对面的昊海楼，旁边的外文书店、二手书店、古籍书店等等……她去医院上班时，我就把随身带的一身衣服留在母亲那里，自己去医院旁边的书店读书。留一身衣服在母亲那里，是怕天热出汗太多没得换吗？我在书店看书，只看不买，这本拿起来看看，那本拿起来翻翻，一上午就过去了。当我下午再去时，有的书店的工作人员就不让我进了，说我只看书，不买书。吃了教训后，下次再去同一个书店看书前，我就换一身衣服再进去，避免让店员认出来。

When I was in primary school, books as plentiful, cheap and easy to get as today's were unimaginable. In the summer holidays I often went to work with my mother, who worked at a hospital in Haidian, Beijing. In the 1990s the hospital's old site stood where the Haidian book mall is now; at first there was only one Xinhua bookshop, then, one after another, the Haohai building opposite, the foreign-language bookshop next door, a second-hand shop, an antiquarian shop… When she went in to work, I left a spare set of clothes with her and went off to the bookshops beside the hospital to read. Why the spare clothes — in case I sweated through the first set in the heat? I read in the shops without buying: pick this one up, leaf through that one, and a whole morning was gone. When I came back in the afternoon, some shops would not let me in — you only read, they said, you never buy. Having learned my lesson, the next time I went to the same shop I changed clothes first, so the staff would not recognise me.

后来上了中学，每次去看书，妈妈就给我 10 元钱，可以用于买自己想看的书。那 10 元钱，我花得非常谨慎，往往在多本书之间反复比较。当时我年纪小，还不知道应该比较哪本书最有价值读，而是看在相同的价位上，哪一本书最厚？哪一本书的字最多？我记得曾经买到过一本不知道哪个版本的雨果的《笑面人》，文字极小，书页极厚。我交了钱，捧着砖头一样沉甸甸的书，咂摸着舌头，觉得自己赚到了。

In secondary school, each time I went to read, my mother gave me ten yuan to buy a book I wanted. I spent those ten yuan with great care, going back and forth between several books. I was too young to know that what I should compare was which book was most worth reading; instead I compared, at the same price, which book was thickest — which had the most words? I remember buying some edition of Hugo's The Man Who Laughs, with tiny print and enormously thick pages. I paid, hugged the brick-heavy volume, and smacked my lips at the bargain I had got.

上大学后，每年的地坛书市我都会去逛一逛，去的时候可以坐公交车，回来的时候就不得不打车了，原因是买书太多，扛不动了。工作之后财务独立，更有了购书的底气，在经常流连于学校图书馆和国家图书馆的同时，我也差不多每个月平均花费大约三分之一的工资用于买书。老西城教研中心的地址在西城区西四北五条胡同里，每次教研结束，我就钻到对面的小胡同里逛打折卖书的小书店。后来新教研中心搬到了现在的新街口，我每次都会拐进路过的中国书店逛上一逛，本着“贼不走空”的原则，每次怎么都要买上一两本。

At university I went to the Ditan book fair every year — by bus on the way there, and by taxi on the way back, because I had bought more than I could carry. Once I was working and financially independent, I bought with even more confidence: while haunting the school library and the National Library, I also spent about a third of each month's salary on books. The old district teaching-research centre sat in a hutong off Xisi in Xicheng; after every meeting I slipped into the lane opposite to browse the little discount bookshops. When the centre moved to Xinjiekou, I turned into the China Bookstore on the way past every single time, on the principle that a thief never leaves empty-handed — one or two books had to come home with me.

终于，在一次搬家之前，我鼓足勇气清理了我的大部分纸质书，包括那本我曾经觉得占了便宜、但最终也没有读完的《笑面人》。几拨朋友来到我家，在旧书堆中拿走他们想要的，剩下的旧书，我当废纸卖了一万多块钱。如此生活 30 年，我的大脑好像并没有像卷帙浩繁的藏书那样无所不知。现在，在书店或者线上看到好书，依然手痒，也经常冲动消费地“购书如山倒”，但“读书如抽丝”的我现在已经深刻地明白了：“藏”书不等于“读”书。

At last, before one move, I gathered the courage to clear out most of my paper books — including that Man Who Laughs I had once thought such a bargain and never finished. Several waves of friends came and took what they wanted from the piles; the rest I sold as scrap paper, for more than ten thousand yuan. After thirty years of living like this, my brain had somehow not become as all-knowing as those sprawling shelves. Even now, a good book in a shop or online makes my fingers itch, and I still buy on impulse — “buying books like a landslide”. But I, who read “like drawing silk from a cocoon”, have finally, deeply understood: owning books is not reading books.

让我终于鼓足勇气去处理纸质藏书的前提是，我已经习惯了阅读电子书。两年“线上教育”硕士项目的学习，从入学 Orientation 到毕业典礼，我连校园都一步没有踏入过。大洋彼岸的教授们通过学习平台给我提供了所有的阅读学习材料；上课、讨论、答疑则通过视频会议完成；教师的教学内容都放在讲解平台上，我可以随时点开学习，加快播放或者慢速播放，重看或者略过。电子书配上有声书边听边读，是我目前的读书方式。

What finally gave me the courage to deal with the paper books was that I had already grown used to reading electronically. In two years of an online master's programme in education, from orientation to graduation, I never set foot on the campus. Professors across the ocean supplied every reading through the learning platform; classes, discussions and office hours ran over video calls; the lectures sat on a narration platform I could open at any time, speed up or slow down, replay or skip. An e-book with its audiobook, listening as I read, is how I read now.

选择的本质是放弃To choose is to give up 必读 · 约 2 分钟Required · ~2 min

北京的三联书店 24 小时开放，上海书城物美价廉，深圳图书馆自由进出……地铁上，候机厅里，咖啡店中……每个人都在翻书，所有的人都捧着手机不停地看。阅读是我们大脑的本能，就像消化吸收食物是我们消化系统的本能一样。你不吃饭，消化系统就告诉你：它饿了，必须进食，甚至吃什么都不重要，先吃饱再说。高糖、高脂肪的食物是更容易选择的，它们在肠胃中先入为主，留给健康食物的空间就不多了。所以，我们才看到，在物质生活水平稳步上升、温饱已经不成问题的当今社会，高血糖、高血脂和高血压这些“富贵病”成了人们新的苦恼——我们有的吃了，我们不饿了，但我们只选择口味，不在乎营养，所以我们吃病了。

Beijing's Sanlian bookshop is open twenty-four hours; Shanghai's Book City is cheap and good; Shenzhen's library lets anyone walk in… On the metro, in the departure lounge, in the café… everyone is turning pages — everyone is staring, without pause, at a phone. Reading is an instinct of the brain, just as digesting food is an instinct of the gut. Skip a meal and the gut tells you: I am hungry, I must eat — and what I eat hardly matters, just fill me first. Sugary, fatty food is the easier choice; it gets into the stomach first and leaves little room for anything healthy. That is why, in a society where living standards have risen steadily and nobody goes hungry, the “diseases of affluence” — high blood sugar, high blood fat, high blood pressure — have become the new complaint: we have food, we are not hungry, but we choose only for taste and never for nourishment, and so we eat ourselves ill.

大脑也是一样的，它得不到信息的输入，也会“饿”，逼着你去给它“喂”一些信息。是什么信息不重要，先输入再说。曾经街头巷尾的看热闹、“东家长，李家短”的刺探，和今天刷抖音、追八卦并无二致。在人们的大脑被垃圾信息先入为主之后，就没有兴趣和时间再拿起好书去读了。

The brain is the same. Deprived of information it too grows “hungry” and forces you to “feed” it something — anything, so long as something goes in. The old craning at street-corner commotions, the prying into whose family did what, is no different from today's scrolling through short videos and chasing gossip. Once the brain has been filled first with junk, there is neither the interest nor the time left to pick up a good book.

应对“三高”的身体健康问题，可以从选择健康膳食出发；应对垃圾信息过载，可以从选择优质学习资源入手。选择的本质是放弃，当你开始选择优质的学习资源时，就开始逐渐远离了八卦和热闹。时间越是有限，“选择”就变得愈加重要，而“选择能力”则成了现代区分不同段位学习者的标尺。同样的一个小时，在不同的书籍面前，我应该翻看哪一本？人生苦短，选择了一个，就意味着放弃了另一个。时间是排他的：看这本，其他的就有可能还没看过，就上了废品收购员的秤。在那一个小时中，选择拿起一本书的同时，本质上就是放弃了另外的所有本。

Against the “three highs” of the body, one can begin by choosing a healthy diet; against the overload of junk information, one can begin by choosing good learning resources. To choose is, in essence, to give up: the moment you start choosing good resources, you begin to drift away from gossip and noise. The more limited the time, the more “choosing” matters — and the ability to choose has become the yardstick that separates learners of different grades today. In the same hour, before different books, which one should I open? Life is short; to choose one is to give up another. Time is exclusive: read this one, and the others may go, unread, onto the scrap dealer's scales. In that hour, to pick up one book is, in essence, to put down every other.

何处惹尘埃Where would the dust settle? 选读 · 约 3 分钟 · 不计入Optional · ~3 min · not counted

知道很多道理，但依然过不好这一生？互联网的发展，终于在阅读资源上实现了“教育机会的平等”。随便打开古登堡网站[4]，你就可以免费翻阅往圣先贤的智慧结晶；登录互联网百科全书，这个世界上最博学的“人”就变成了你垂手而立的家庭教师……可新的挑战也随之而来：你选择在古登堡网站上翻看哪一本？你怎么将互联网百科全书上的知识变成你的学识？

Knowing so many truths, yet still not living well? The growth of the Internet has at last brought “equal opportunity” to reading. Open Project Gutenberg[4] and the distilled wisdom of the sages is yours for nothing; log on to an online encyclopedia and the most learned “person” in the world stands at your elbow as a private tutor… But new challenges arrive with them: which book on Gutenberg do you choose to open? How do you turn the encyclopedia's knowledge into your own learning?

编者注：本版此段略去一句。Editor's note: one sentence of this paragraph is omitted in this edition.

当“教育的温饱”不再成问题之后，人们的学习如何“奔小康”？这是我们当下的挑战。不论是学术论文还是新闻报道，都在用数据和事实告诉我们：互联网网路广开，线上课程免费提供，教学资源无处不在的同时，人与人之间的教育不公平现象不是缓和了，而是加剧了[5]；农村留守儿童的手机沉迷现象越来越严重了[6]；甚至线上学习的阶层划分也随之出现了……

Once “educational subsistence” is no longer a problem, how does learning move on to “modest prosperity”? That is our present challenge. Research papers and news reports alike tell us, with data and facts, that while the web is wide open, online courses are free and teaching resources are everywhere, educational inequality between people has not eased but sharpened[5]; phone addiction among rural left-behind children grows worse[6]; and even online learning has begun to divide into classes…

解法在哪里？从苏格拉底和孔子的时代到现在，针对每一代学习者的挑战，其实从来都没变过：从“要我学”，到“我要学”，再到“我会学”，最后到“我善学”。我常常和校内的老师们说：让你的学生珍视阅读，精通选书，善于阅读，你的教育教学工作就已经做到合格了；如果你还能教会学生“想得透，表达得清，做得到”，那你的教育教学工作就已经做到优秀了；如果你甚至还能教会他们从 0 到 1 的创造，那么你的教育教学工作就堪称卓越了。

Where is the answer? From the age of Socrates and Confucius to now, the challenge facing each generation of learners has never really changed: from “I am made to learn”, to “I want to learn”, to “I know how to learn”, and finally to “I learn well”. I often tell the teachers at school: if your students treasure reading, choose books well and read well, your teaching is already adequate; if you can also teach them to think it through, say it clearly and get it done, your teaching is excellent; and if you can even teach them to create from nothing, your teaching is outstanding.

从莎草纸到羊皮卷，从甲骨文到竹简，从印刷机到互联网，从纸质书到电子书……相信我，信息的载体依然会持续改进下去，学习的场景会不停地发展。我们应该在随变的时代洪流中，选择抓牢那永恒不变的学习精髓，放飞自己对于学习形式的想象力，打开视野和认知的自我局限，避免自己饿死在富饶之地。“菩提本无树，明镜亦非台，本来无一物，何处惹尘埃？”这句佛家箴言，倒是说出了信息过载时代的学习正解：在书林学海之上，观念的选择、习惯的养成、榜样的力量……虽然无形，却更能够从本质上帮助学习者去伪存真，平心静气，持之以恒，积极乐观地通往“自由学习之路”。微笑着做到下一个小时比上一个小时更好一点，明天比今天更好一些，下一周的自己会是更好的自己……在旁人焦虑、盲从、躺平的时候，你选择“慢慢”变好，“慢慢”变聪明，“慢慢”变强大——因为你懂得，这“慢慢”二字，也意味着你将更长久地拥有这些美好。

From papyrus to parchment, from oracle bones to bamboo slips, from the printing press to the Internet, from paper to e-books… believe me, the carriers of information will keep improving, and the scenes of learning will keep changing. In the shifting flood of the times we should choose to hold fast to the unchanging essence of learning, set our imagination free about the forms it takes, open the self-imposed limits of our vision and understanding — and avoid starving in a land of plenty. “Bodhi is no tree, the mirror no stand; there is nothing there at all — where would the dust settle?” That Buddhist verse speaks, oddly, the truth about learning in an age of overload: above the forest of books and the sea of learning, the choice of ideas, the forming of habits, the power of examples… invisible, yet far more able to help a learner tell true from false, keep calm, persist, and walk with optimism the road to free learning. Smiling, make the next hour a little better than the last, tomorrow a little better than today, next week's self a better self… While others fret, follow the crowd, or lie flat, you choose to get better slowly, wiser slowly, stronger slowly — because you understand that “slowly” also means you will keep these good things for far longer.
