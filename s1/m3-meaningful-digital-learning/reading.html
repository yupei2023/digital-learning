10 给线上学习者的四个锦囊10 Four tips for the online learner 必读 · 约 5 分钟Required · ~5 min

本章导读Chapter at a glance

讲完三项修炼，作者送给线上学习者四个“锦囊”——下面这张图是它们的全貌：四只并列，中间是拆它们的人。本单元真正要练的是被标出来的那两只；读的时候留意作者怎么把“时间”和“流程”讲成两件当天就能动手做的事。

Having covered the three disciplines, the author hands the online learner four “tips” — the figure below holds all four at once, side by side, with the person who opens them in the middle. This module practises the two picked out in colour; as you read, watch how the author turns “time” and “routine” into two things you could do the same day.

讲完了线上学习的三项修炼，我再送给各位朋友四个锦囊。

Having spoken about the three disciplines of online learning, let me hand you four tips — four “brocade pouches”, as the old stories call the sealed advice a strategist gives to be opened at the right moment.

第一个锦囊：为你所用

The first tip: make it serve you

线上的优质资源太多了，我们一生也无法穷尽。早在互联网出现之前，庄子就对学习资料太多而人的精力有限发出过精妙的感叹：“吾生也有涯，而知也无涯。以有涯随无涯，殆已。”我们在线上学习时，面对的是比庄子那个时代丰富得多的知识汪洋。这个时候，我们要避免积攒太多而学得太少：看到这个课程好，我报个名；看到那个资料好，我下载下来……报名课程、把资料存进硬盘，这种学习意识是好的，但你报了课程，不等于你学到了本领；你下载了资料，不等于你读到了有意义的内容。相反，如果过度在意获取更多的资料，把更多的注意力都放在生怕错过更好的内容上，只会让你产生更多的压力和不必要的焦虑。资料，只有用了，才是你的；课程，只有学了，才是你的。请记住：世间万物，非你所有，为你所用。

There are far more good resources online than one life can exhaust. Long before the Internet, Zhuangzi sighed elegantly at the mismatch between endless material and limited human energy: “My life has a limit, but knowledge has none; to pursue the limitless with the limited is perilous.” The ocean of knowledge we face online is vastly richer than anything in Zhuangzi's day. That is exactly when we must avoid hoarding much and learning little: this course looks good — I'll enrol; that file looks good — I'll download it… Enrolling and saving files show a good instinct, but enrolling in a course is not learning a skill, and downloading a file is not reading anything meaningful. On the contrary, fussing over getting more, and fretting about missing something better, only breeds pressure and needless anxiety. A resource is yours only once you use it; a course is yours only once you learn it. Remember: the things of this world are not yours to own, but yours to use.

第二个锦囊：时间合理

The second tip: keep time reasonable

在线上学习的过程中，我们有时会感到时间流逝得特别快。你可能从一个问题出发，一边检索，一边跳到了另一个问题上；为了解决那个问题，你又需要检索另外一些资料，于是又跳到了别的资料上；接着，你发现在读这份资料的过程中有个概念不理解，就去查这个概念是什么意思；查概念的时候，可能又看到一条突发新闻，然后你又去看这条新闻。等绕了一大圈，好几个小时可能都过去了，甚至，你已经忘了最初要解决的问题是什么了。

When learning online we sometimes feel time slipping away unusually fast. You start from one question, search, and jump to another; to settle that one you need other material, so you jump again; reading it, you meet a term you don't understand and look it up; while looking it up you spot a piece of breaking news and go and read that. One large loop later, several hours may have passed — and you may have forgotten what you set out to solve.

这是人们在线上学习时非常容易出现的一种现象，就好像随波逐流一样——怪不得人们把上网叫做“网上冲浪”呢。那我们要怎样应对这种现象呢？我们要把时间合理地安排起来，专时专用：这四十分钟，就是用于线上学习的；每天到了这个时间点，就必须开始线上学习；一旦四十分钟到了，才可以再去查看别的网上信息。一旦线上学习形成了习惯，习惯会帮你节省更多的心力。

This happens very easily online; it is like drifting with the current — no wonder people call it “surfing”. How do we deal with it? By arranging time sensibly and giving each time its purpose: these forty minutes are for online learning; when this time comes each day, learning begins; only when the forty minutes are up may you look at anything else on the web. Once online learning has become a habit, the habit spares you a great deal of effort.

第三个锦囊：流程操作

The third tip: run it as a routine

在线学习不像玩在线游戏，也不像在线刷剧，它并不是一件轻松的事情。我见过很多在线学习者，刚开始一个在线学习项目的时候目光炯炯、兴致盎然，可是一两周、一两个月之后，新鲜劲儿没了，精力不足了，甚至中途放弃。如果你把自己的线上学习活动，与你感兴趣的或者比较有规律的活动进行连锁式的流程设置，也许能更好地帮你持续不断地进行在线学习。事情一旦进入一个流程，你只要开启了它，就更容易进入一种自动进行的状态。比如，我把自己的线上学习与喝咖啡这件事联系在一起：开始在线学习之前，先喝一杯咖啡。喝咖啡对我来说是一个放松休闲的活动，喝完咖啡，我就会自动进入打开电脑、进行线上学习的流程之中。也因为这样的流程设置，我平常喝咖啡的量比较多。各位也可以根据自己的实际情况，把在线学习与你喜欢的活动设置成连锁流程，让轻松休闲的活动带着在线学习一起进行。

Online learning is not like online gaming or binge-watching; it is not effortless. I have seen many online learners begin a programme bright-eyed and eager, only to lose the novelty and the energy after a week or two, or a month or two, and quit halfway. If you chain your online learning to an activity you enjoy or already do regularly, it may help you keep going. Once something belongs to a routine, starting the routine tends to carry it along automatically. For instance, I tie online learning to coffee: before I study, I drink a cup. Coffee, for me, is relaxation; when the cup is empty I slide automatically into opening the laptop and beginning — which is also why I drink rather a lot of coffee. You can do the same with whatever suits you: chain online learning to an activity you like, and let the easy, pleasant activity carry the learning along with it.

第四个锦囊：社群参与

The fourth tip: join a community

我曾经有很长一段时间特别喜欢去图书馆学习。尤其是假期一到、周末一到，在家里太舒服了，躺在床上看看电影，一天时间很快就滑过去了；而同样的周末，我如果到了图书馆，就会在那里自动开始看书学习，因为周围全都是看书和学习的人。在这样的环境里，我自然而然地就想珍惜时间学习了。现在，我们在线上可以更方便地组建自己的学习社群，邀请几个爱学习的好朋友，在社群里定期分享读书感悟、学习心得；也可以寻找由专人组织的学习社群，这样的社群会提供更专业的服务和分享。在这样的社群里，你会受到环境的影响，不知不觉地就学到了很多东西。要想在社群里收获更大，就需要主动为社群做贡献，比如积极分享自己的学习心得、提出好的问题、帮助社群成员解答问题等。这叫做“躬身入局”：当你把社群里的学习活动与自己建立起联系，你会更主动地调用更多的精力进行学习，这样一来，你也会在同样的时间里学到更多的知识。

For a long stretch I loved studying in libraries. Especially on holidays and weekends, home is too comfortable: lie on the bed, watch a film, and the day slides away. On the same weekend, if I went to the library, I would automatically start reading and studying, because everyone around me was reading and studying. In that environment I naturally wanted to make the most of the time. Today it is easier still to form a learning community online: invite a few friends who love learning and share reading notes and study insights regularly, or find a community run by professionals, which offers more expert help and sharing. In such a community the environment works on you, and you learn a great deal without noticing. To gain more from it, contribute to it — share what you have learned, ask good questions, help others with theirs. This is called “stepping into the game”: once the community's learning is tied to you, you summon more of your own energy, and learn more in the same time.

本课程的说明：这门课不设小组和讨论区，你的作品和反思都是个人的。第四个锦囊请理解为“把自己放进有人在学习的环境里”——图书馆、自习室、或者和家人约定的一个安静时段——而不是加入某个群。

A note for this course: this course has no groups and no forum; your work and reflections are your own. Read the fourth tip as “put yourself among people who are learning” — a library, a study room, a quiet slot agreed with your family — rather than joining a chat group.

独学而无友，孤陋而寡闻。积极组建社群、积极参与社群，可以让自己拥有更多的榜样；同时，通过自己做榜样，你将变得更加出色。

“To study alone, without friends, is to be narrow and ill-informed.” Building and joining communities gives you more models to learn from — and by being a model yourself, you become better still.

以上就是我分享给各位在线学习者的四个锦囊，希望大家边学边用。

Those are my four tips for the online learner. I hope you will use them as you learn.

高效在线学习者的三项修炼：运用逻辑、勤做检索、多语对照。作者先说，三项修炼都以“主动学习”为前提——“求学”的“求”字，比所有秘诀都宝贵。本单元只要求读“多语对照”一节：用中文学完一个概念，再用英文学一遍，不仅复习巩固，有时还能解开中文名字造成的误会（有理数、动脉血）。另外两项修炼，第二学期第 10 单元会深读。

Three disciplines of the effective online learner: use logic, search diligently, read in two languages. The author first insists all three rest on one precondition — active learning: the “seeking” in “seeking knowledge” is worth more than every trick. This module requires only the section on two languages: learn a concept in Chinese, then again in English — a review that sometimes also dissolves misunderstandings created by the Chinese name (rational numbers, arterial blood). The other two disciplines are read in depth in Semester 2, Module 10.

通过上一期节目，我们知道了：互联网可以让我们很方便地触达信息，但也对我们辨别信息的能力提出了更高的要求；互联网让我们的学习变得更自由，也同时要求我们具有更强的时间规划能力；互联网给我们的学习提供了更多的可能性，这也要求我们有更强的计划性和相应的专注能力。这一期节目，我就和大家聊一聊，想要成为高效的在线学习者所需要的三项修炼。

From the previous episode we learned that the Internet lets us reach information easily but demands more of our ability to judge it; that it makes learning freer but demands stronger time planning; that it offers more possibilities for learning but demands more discipline in planning and focus. In this episode I want to talk about the three disciplines you need to become an effective online learner.

首先，我要对正在听这期节目的朋友表示祝贺，因为在众多节目中选择这个节目来听的朋友，已经具备了线上学习的前提条件，那就是学习的主动性。我后面要分享的这三项修炼，如果没有您主动学习这个前提，一个也不可能发挥作用。其实，无论是线上学习还是线下学习，求学的“求”字——也就是主动学习这个前提，都比所有的秘诀更宝贵。针对各位已经开始主动学习的线上学习者，我建议大家经常要做的修炼是：运用逻辑、勤做检索和多语对照。下面我分别给大家详细介绍一下。

First, congratulations: by choosing this episode among so many, you have already shown the one precondition of online learning — initiative. Without that, none of the three disciplines I am about to share can do anything. In truth, online or offline, the “seeking” in “seeking knowledge” — the precondition of active learning — is worth more than every trick put together. For those of you already learning actively, the disciplines I recommend practising regularly are: use logic, search diligently, and read in two languages side by side. Let me take them in turn.

第三项修炼：多语对照

The third discipline: two languages side by side

在各位进行互联网信息检索和线上学习的过程中，常常使用多语对照，不仅能让我们更加有效地辨别信息，还有益于我们的学习效果。这里的多语，至少是我们使用最熟练的母语中文，和互联网上使用最广泛的语言英语。

Whether searching the web or learning online, setting two languages side by side not only helps you judge information more effectively but also improves learning itself. “Two languages” means, at minimum, the one we command best — Chinese — and the one used most widely on the Internet — English.

编者注：下一段的“刚才我们在说野鸡大学”指上面折叠的选读段“勤做检索”；本章原是音频节目的文稿，“节目”就是“这一章”。Editor's note: “diploma mills, as we were just saying” refers to the folded optional section “search diligently” above; this chapter began as a podcast script, so “episode” means “chapter”.

先来说说辨别信息。刚才我们在说野鸡大学的时候，如果大家用多语对照，就可以直接避开一些陷阱。比如，有一所野鸡大学叫“西太平洋大学”，英文名字是 Western Pacific University。在中文名字上，它容易让人和另外一所“西太平洋大学”混淆——这所学校的中文全名叫西雅图太平洋大学，是一所 1891 年在美国华盛顿州西雅图建立的正规私立大学，英文名字是 Seattle Pacific University。如果你仅用中文，就会被两所同样叫做“西太平洋大学”的学校搞晕；但当你使用多语对照的时候，就可以轻松地识别出两者之间的差异了。

First, judging information. When we spoke of diploma mills, two languages side by side would have let you sidestep some traps outright. One diploma mill calls itself “西太平洋大学” — Western Pacific University. In Chinese it is easily confused with another “西太平洋大学”, whose full Chinese name is 西雅图太平洋大学 — Seattle Pacific University, a legitimate private university founded in Seattle, Washington, in 1891. Using Chinese alone, the two “West Pacific Universities” blur into one; set the English beside the Chinese and the difference is obvious.

再来看看多语对照是怎样帮我们提升学习效果的。

Now, how two languages improve learning.

比如，数学中有“无理数”和“有理数”这样两个概念。当年我在学习这两个概念的时候就一头懵：为什么有的数字有道理，有的数字无道理呢？这样想，就是我们只用中文学习时可能会进入的误区。有理数，英文是 rational number。Rational 这个英文单词的拉丁文词源是 ratio，意思是“比”——比例的那个比。后来，数学家用 rational number 表示能够写成两个整数之比的数。Rational 在现代英语中，还有“有道理的”意思。我国最早翻译 rational number 的译者进行翻译的时候，是根据现代英语中 rational“有道理的”这个意思来考虑的，同时也参考了日语中“有理数”的日文汉字翻译，把它译成了“有理数”。于是，无法写成两个整数之比的 irrational number，就顺着翻译为“无理数”了。所以，如果从英文的角度来帮我们理解有理数和无理数，那么有理数就是可以表示成两个整数之比的数，而无理数就是无法写成两个整数之比的数。这样就更容易理解了。

Take “rational” and “irrational” numbers — in Chinese, 有理数 and 无理数, literally “numbers with reason” and “numbers without reason”. When I first met them I was baffled: how can a number have or lack reason? That confusion is a trap you fall into only when learning in Chinese alone. The Latin root of rational is ratio — a ratio, as in proportion. Mathematicians used rational number for a number that can be written as a ratio of two integers. In modern English rational also means “reasonable”, and the first Chinese translators, working from that modern sense and from the Japanese rendering, produced 有理数 — and irrational number, the number that cannot be written as a ratio of integers, duly became 无理数. Seen through the English, then: a rational number is one that can be expressed as a ratio of two integers; an irrational number is one that cannot. Much easier.

再比如，中学生物课上，有的学生花了很大的力气，也无法分清静脉、静脉血、动脉、动脉血这几个概念之间的关系，很容易被这四个概念绕晕，总觉得静脉中的血液是静脉血，动脉中的血液是动脉血——没办法，它们的中文名字实在是太像了。这时，只需要加入英文名词的解释，就直接降低了理解难度：动脉（Artery），静脉（Vein），动脉血（Oxygen-rich Blood，富氧血），静脉血（Oxygen-poor Blood，贫氧血）。好了，现在你只需要认识 rich 是富有、poor 是贫穷，就能知道 oxygen-rich blood 是富氧血，这是动脉血的本质；oxygen-poor blood 是贫氧血，这是静脉血的本质——它们和动脉、静脉本身没有任何关系。同时，用英文来重新观察这四个事物，绝对不会不自觉地把“动脉”和“动脉血”联系起来，因为在英文中，artery、vein 和 oxygen-rich blood、oxygen-poor blood 这些词汇之间有着天差地别，不会产生名词上的混淆。所以，理解不同血管和不同血液之间的关系，也就更容易了。

Another example, from school biology: some students struggle endlessly to separate 静脉 (vein), 静脉血 (“vein blood”), 动脉 (artery) and 动脉血 (“artery blood”), and end up assuming the blood in veins must be “vein blood” and the blood in arteries “artery blood” — the Chinese names are simply too alike. Add the English and the difficulty collapses: artery, vein, oxygen-rich blood (富氧血), oxygen-poor blood (贫氧血). Now you need only know that rich means abundant and poor means scarce: oxygen-rich blood is the essence of “arterial blood”, oxygen-poor blood the essence of “venous blood” — and neither has anything to do with the vessel names. In English, artery, vein, oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor are worlds apart, so no confusion of names arises, and the relation between vessels and blood becomes easy to grasp.

在线学习的一大便利，就是可以快速地同时调用多方资料。当你用中文学习完一个概念之后，可以尝试用英文再学一遍，不仅能帮你复习巩固，有时还能辅助你更深入地理解概念。

One great convenience of online learning is that many sources can be summoned at once. After learning a concept in Chinese, try learning it again in English: it consolidates the review, and sometimes deepens the understanding itself.

“有意义的学习”不是看过、记住，而是能拿它做点什么。费曼的方法把这句话变成一个可操作的动作：讲给别人听。读的时候试试。Meaningful learning is not having seen or memorised something — it is being able to do something with it. Feynman turns that into one concrete move: explain it to someone. Try it as you read.
