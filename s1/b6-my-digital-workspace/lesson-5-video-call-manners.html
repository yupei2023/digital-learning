一对一视频通话礼仪The manners of a one-to-one video call

这门课全程自学，但你并不孤单：课程提供预约制的一对一视频答疑——卡住了、想当面问，就约一段和导师或教师的视频通话（预约方式随开课信公布，见第 7 周页面的常见问题）。视频通话是你数字工作台上"人"的那一面：设备是工具，得体是手艺。本课把这门手艺备好，将来求职面试、远程课堂，用的都是同一套。

This course is self-paced throughout, but you are not alone in it: it offers bookable one-to-one video help sessions — when you are stuck and want to ask in person, you book a call with your mentor or the instructor (the booking route is published with the welcome letter; see Week 7's FAQ). A video call is the human face of your digital workspace: the gear is a tool, the conduct is a craft. This lesson stocks that craft — and the same set serves future job interviews and remote classrooms unchanged.

提前五分钟：四件小事Five minutes early: four small things

  1. 试设备：麦克风、摄像头、网络各试一下（多数会议软件有"设备检测"入口）。设备翻车不是丢人的事，但完全可以提前五分钟避免。Test the gear: microphone, camera, connection (most meeting apps carry a device-check entry). Gear failure is no disgrace — merely a thing five early minutes fully prevents.
  2. 选位置：安静、不逆光（别背对窗户坐）。看一眼镜头里的背景：家里的门牌、快递单、家人照片，都是隐私——挪开，或用软件的虚拟背景。Choose the spot: quiet, not backlit (never sit with a window behind you). Then check the frame's background: door plates, courier slips, family photos are all private information — move them, or use the app's virtual background.
  3. 备材料：要问的问题一句话写好，配上标注过的截图（第 2 周的手艺——"怎样提一个带截图的问题"在答疑里价值连城），放在手边。Ready the materials: each question in one written line, its annotated screenshot beside it (Week 2's craft — "how to ask a question with a screenshot" is worth gold in a help call), all within reach.
  4. 穿着日常得体：不需要正装，像去学校那样穿就正好。Dress ordinarily and decently: no formal wear needed — what you would wear to school is exactly right.

通话之中：五条规矩During the call: five rules

  • 进门先静音：进入会议默认闭麦，轮到你说再开——这是线上世界的"进门换鞋"。Enter muted: join with the microphone off and open it when your turn comes — the online world's shoes-off-at-the-door.
  • 问好之后直接说事：一对一答疑不是面试，不需要开场白。"老师好，我卡在这一步——您看这张截图"就是最好的开场。Greet, then straight to the matter: a one-to-one help call is no interview and wants no speech. "Hello — I'm stuck at this step; here's my screenshot" is the finest opening there is.
  • 听完再接：网络有半秒延迟，抢话会撞车；对方说完停一拍再开口。Let them finish: the network runs half a second behind, and interjections collide; wait a beat after they stop before you start.
  • 卡顿了照直说："刚才卡住了，能再说一遍吗？"不失礼——假装听见了才误事。掉线就重新进入，进来说声"抱歉，掉线了"即可。Name the glitch plainly: "it froze just now — could you say that again?" is perfectly polite; pretending to have heard is what causes the damage. Dropped? Rejoin with "sorry, lost the connection" and carry on.
  • 不录制对方：未经对方同意不录音录屏——第 5 周你不愿别人拿走你的信息，同一条金规矩反过来也成立。结束时道声谢，顺手把答案记进笔记（微课 3 的落点正等着它）。Never record the other person: no audio or screen recording without their consent — Week 5's golden rule about your information, turned the other way around, holds just as firmly. Close with thanks, and file the answer into your notes (micro-lesson 3's landing place is waiting for it).

诚实的说明书：礼仪清单不是考卷。迟到了两分钟、忘了静音被听见了背景音、名字忘了改——一句"抱歉"加上改正，就是完全得体的补救；得体的补救本身就是礼仪的一部分（这句话你在第 2 周的邮件课听过——同一个道理，换了个房间）。The honest manual: this list is no exam paper. Two minutes late, a mute button forgotten, a display name left unchanged — one "sorry" plus the fix is entirely graceful recovery; graceful recovery is itself part of the manners (you heard this in Week 2's email lesson — the same truth, in a different room).
