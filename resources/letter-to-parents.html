A Letter to Parents

关于《数字化学习》课程 · About the Digital Learning course

日期 Date：【请填写 Fill in：落款日期 date of this letter】

尊敬的家长：

Dear Parents,

我是您孩子在《数字化学习》课程中的导师。请允许我先说一句心里话：把孩子交给一门”没有课堂、没有铃声、没有人在旁边盯着”的课，需要信任。这封信就是为这份信任而写的——我想让您清楚地知道，这门课要带孩子去哪里、每一周他会做些什么、我们怎样保护他，以及在这一年里，您能怎样实实在在地帮上忙。

I am your child’s mentor in Digital Learning. Let me begin with something honest: entrusting your child to a course with no classroom, no bell, and nobody standing over their shoulder takes trust. This letter is written for that trust. I want you to know exactly where this course is taking your child, what they will do each week, how we protect them, and — concretely — how you can help over the year ahead.

这封信不需要您回复，也不需要签字。读完它大约需要十分钟。如果您只有三分钟，请直接翻到第六节《家长可以怎样帮忙》和第八节《我们的三个请求》——那两节是这封信真正的重点。

You do not need to reply to this letter or sign anything. Reading it takes about ten minutes. If you only have three, please turn straight to Section 6 (How you can help) and Section 8 (Three things we ask of you) — those two sections are what this letter is really for.

一、这是一门什么课，为什么要有它

1. What this course is, and why it exists

孩子这一代人，几乎所有的知识都可以在网上找到。但”找得到”和”学得会”之间，隔着一段很长的距离。他们能熟练地刷视频、切换应用、在游戏里配合无间，却常常不知道怎样为自己定一个学习目标、怎样判断一条信息是真是假、怎样在没有人监督的时候把一件事做完。他们是数码世界的原住民，却未必是数码世界里合格的学习者。 这两件事完全不一样。

Your child’s generation can find almost any knowledge online. But there is a long distance between finding and learning. They swipe through videos, switch apps, and coordinate flawlessly inside a game — yet often do not know how to set a learning goal for themselves, how to judge whether a claim is true, or how to finish something when nobody is watching. They are natives of the digital world, but that does not make them competent learners in it. These are two entirely different things.

这门课教的不是某一款软件，而是怎样学习——尤其是怎样在线上、独自一人的情况下，把学习真正做成。第一学期我们打地基：“站稳脚跟，学会学习”：写一封得体的邮件、截图和录屏、用翻译工具读英文、管好账号和密码、检索并记录来源、搭好自己的数字工作台；然后进入”什么是学习”“什么是数字化学习”“怎样让学习真正发生”这些更根本的问题。第二学期走向”判断、创造、自主”：判断网上信息的可信度、管理自己的数字足迹与数字健康、和家人一起商定家庭媒体公约、为下一届的同学做一节微课，最后独立完成一个属于自己的毕业项目。

This course does not teach a piece of software. It teaches how to learn — especially how to make learning actually happen online, alone. Semester 1 lays the foundation, “getting equipped, learning to learn”: writing a proper email, taking screenshots and screen recordings, reading English with translation tools, managing accounts and passwords, searching and recording sources, and setting up a personal digital workspace. From there we move to the deeper questions: what learning is, what digital learning is, and how to make learning actually happen. Semester 2 turns to “judging, creating, self-directing”: weighing the credibility of online information, managing one’s digital footprint and wellbeing, agreeing a family media agreement at home, making a micro-lesson for next year’s students, and finally completing an independent capstone project.

打个比方：这门课有点像在孩子出远门之前，陪他把行李、地图和常识准备好——数码世界对许多家长来说是一个陌生的国度，语言不同、规矩不同，路上既有丰饶的资源，也有隐蔽的陷阱。这个比方有它不准的地方： 陌生国度可以不去，也可以买张机票回来；数码世界不行。孩子早已生活在里面，我们无法把他拦在门外，只能把他培养成一个能在里面照顾好自己、并且学得会东西的人。而且严格说来，您也并不站在国境线外送行——您和他在同一个国度里，只是您到得晚一些。

An analogy: this course is a bit like helping a child pack the luggage, the map, and the common sense before a long journey abroad — for many parents the digital world is a foreign country, with its own language and its own customs, full of abundant resources and hidden traps alike. Where the analogy breaks down: a foreign country can be skipped, and you can always fly home. The digital world cannot. Your child already lives there; we cannot keep them outside the gate, only raise someone who can take care of themselves inside it and genuinely learn there. And strictly speaking you are not waving goodbye at the border — you are in the same country, having simply arrived later.

二、这门课怎么运行

2. How the course runs

学制 Length 两个学期，每学期 16 个教学周。Two semesters, 16 teaching weeks each.
形式 Format 完全异步的在线自学——没有固定上课时间，没有直播，不需要孩子在某个时刻出现在某个地方。Fully asynchronous online self-study — no class meetings, no live sessions, no fixed hour at which your child must be somewhere.
周次 Weeks 课程只说”第几周”，不绑定具体日期；遇到法定节假日，当周的任务顺延，国庆周等设为缓冲周，不开新内容。The course speaks in weeks, not dates; on statutory holidays that week’s work rolls forward, and holiday weeks such as National Day are buffer weeks with no new content.
每周时长 Weekly time 必做的学习总量不超过 120 分钟，连外部课程也算在内；标注”选做”的内容一律不计入。Required work never exceeds 120 minutes a week, external course included; anything marked optional is outside that budget.
节奏 Rhythm 每周一开放当周页面；建议周三前看完材料、周六前完成任务、周日前发布并写日志。Pages open Monday; suggested pace is materials by Wednesday, task by Saturday, publish and log by Sunday.
迟交 Late work 没有迟交处罚。 我们只给”建议节奏”，因为下一周不会等他。落后了就先做当周的，再用缓冲周补。There are no late penalties. We give a suggested pace instead, because the next week does not wait. If they fall behind: do the current week first, then catch up in a buffer week.
个体学习 Individual learning 这门课为个体学习者设计：没有小组作业，没有讨论区，没有互相回帖。每一件作品都是孩子自己的，每一周都以他自己的反思收尾。Designed for the individual learner: no group work, no forum, no peer replies. Every artifact is your child’s own, and every week closes with their own reflection.
导师 Mentors 课程有两位导师，各负责约 30 名学生，我是其中之一：我每周检查我这一组每一位学生的进度，按量规评分，邮件反馈，哪一周没交就在当周联系学生；也可预约一对一视频答疑。The course has two mentors, each supporting about 30 students; I am one of them. Every week I check the progress of every student in my group, score their work by rubric, reply by email, contact any student whose work is missing that same week, and offer 1:1 video by appointment.

这里有一句必须说清楚的话：在线课程并不比线下课程轻松。 它省掉的是通勤和坐班，换来的是对自我管理的更高要求。教纲里的建议是：每周固定两个约一小时的时段，专门给这门课。这一点，正是您能帮上最大忙的地方（见第六节）。

One thing must be said plainly: an online course is not the easy option. It saves the commute and the seat time, and asks for far more self-management in exchange. The syllabus recommends reserving two roughly one-hour blocks each week for this course. This is precisely where you can help most (Section 6).

三、孩子的一周，具体是什么样

3. What a week actually looks like

每个教学周只有一张网页——这张页面本身就是这一周的学习，而不是任务的入口。它固定由十个部分组成：

Each teaching week is one web page — and that page is the week’s learning, not a doorway to an assignment. It always has the same ten parts:

  1. 本周目标——学完这周他应该能做到什么。Objectives — what they should be able to do by the end.
  2. 预计用时——看/读多少分钟、做多少分钟、反思多少分钟，加起来不超过 120 分钟，明明白白印在页面上。Time estimate — minutes for watching/reading, for doing, for reflecting, adding up to no more than 120, printed openly on the page.
  3. 材料——文字加一段短视频（视频托管在国内可以直接打开的平台，每段不超过 5 分钟，配中英字幕，并且每段视频都配一份完整文字版，看不了视频也能学）。Materials — text plus a short video (hosted where it opens directly in mainland China, under five minutes, with bilingual captions, and a full text version alongside every video so the week works even if the video does not).
  4. 跟做——一个浏览器里的小练习，即时反馈，不计分。Follow-along — a small in-browser exercise with instant feedback, ungraded.
  5. 本周任务 + 量规 + 自评清单——任务说明、评分标准，以及一份可以打勾的自查清单。The week’s task, its rubric, and a checkable self-review list.
  6. 自测题——不计分、可以反复重做、当场给答案解释，只用来帮他自己看清哪里没懂。Self-quiz — ungraded, retakeable, with instant explanations; it exists only to show them what they have not understood yet.
  7. 反思 · 三行日志——每周在自己网站上写三行：这周我学到了什么 · 哪里卡住了 · 下周我打算怎么办。Reflection · the three-line log — three lines on their own website each week: what I learned · where I got stuck · what I will do next week.
  8. 想学更多？——选做的拓展资源，不计分，不计入那 120 分钟。Want more? — optional extras, ungraded, outside the 120 minutes.
  9. 建议节奏——例如”周三前看完材料，周六前完成任务”。Suggested pace — for example, “materials by Wednesday, task by Saturday”.
  10. 常见问题——孩子最可能卡住的地方，先在页面上回答一遍。FAQ — the places students most often get stuck, answered on the page first.

第 3 周，孩子会用课程指定的建站平台建一个属于自己的个人网站，三个页面：关于我 · 学习日志 · 作品集。从那以后，这个网站就是他的作业本：每一件作品发布在上面，每一周写三行日志。第二学期还会增加”项目”和”我的一年”两页。

In Week 3 your child builds a personal website on the course’s designated builder, with three pages: About me · Learning log · Portfolio. From then on that website is their notebook: every piece of work is published there and every week gets its three-line log. Semester 2 adds two more pages, Project and My year.

第一学期里，孩子还会从一份课程菜单中挑两门公开免费的在线课程真正去学一门（“主修”一门学完，“探索”一门只试两三节）——学会在线学习，最好的办法是真的在线学一门课。 主修课的学习安排在学期后段，每周一小时，这一小时也算在那 120 分钟之内。孩子在注册任何一个外部课程平台之前，我们要求他先告诉您。

During Semester 1 your child also picks two free public online courses from a course menu and actually takes them (one core course to finish, one explore course sampled for two or three lessons) — the best way to learn how to learn online is to really learn something online. The core course runs one hour a week in the later part of the semester, and that hour sits inside the 120 minutes. Before registering on any external course platform, we ask your child to tell you first.

四、作品怎么评，放在哪里

4. How work is assessed, and where it lives

评分不是打对错，而是对着标准看证据。 每一件计分的作品都附一张量规，量规的每一行就是这个单元的学习目标，每一行分四级：

Assessment here is not marking right and wrong; it is reading evidence against a standard. Every graded task comes with a rubric whose rows are that module’s learning objectives, and each row has four levels:

等级 Level 含义 What it means
达标 Meets 作品清楚地做到了这条目标。The work clearly does what the objective asks.
发展中 Developing 方向对了，还差一点火候。On the right track, not there yet.
尚未达标 Not yet 这条目标还没有做到。The objective has not been met yet.
证据不足 No evidence 不是做得不好，而是作品里看不出来——通常是漏了一步，补上就行。Not “poor work” but “nothing to read” — usually a missing step that can simply be added.

请特别留意最后一级：“证据不足”不是评价孩子的能力，而是说这次交上来的东西没能说明问题。这是可以补的，也常常只需要补一句话或一张截图。

Please note that last level in particular. “No evidence” is not a judgement of your child’s ability; it says this submission did not show us enough. That is fixable, and often needs no more than one added sentence or one screenshot.

作品发布在孩子自己的网站上，然后把那一页的链接登记给我。在校学生由我按量规评分并在一周内邮件反馈；每学期满分 100 分，60 分及以上为合格并获学期证书，85 分及以上标注”优秀”，两个学期都合格的孩子另获年度证书。

Work is published on your child’s own website, and the link to that page is registered with me. Enrolled students are scored by me against the rubric and receive email feedback within a week. Each semester is out of 100: 60 or above passes and earns the semester certificate, 85 or above is marked “with distinction”, and passing both semesters earns the year certificate.

课程中还有几次不计分的”学习状态自测”，让孩子在学期初和学期末对照着看自己的变化。它不进成绩，不影响证书，结果只显示在孩子自己的浏览器里。

The course also includes a few ungraded “learning self-checks” so your child can compare themselves at the start and end of a semester. They never enter the grade, never affect the certificate, and the results display only in your child’s own browser.

五、隐私与安全：我们做了哪些保护

5. Privacy and safety: what we have put in place

这是最多家长问到的地方，我把它写得尽量具体：

This is what parents ask about most, so let me be as concrete as I can:

  • 化名，不用真名。 孩子在网站上使用英文名或化名。Pseudonyms, not real names. Your child uses an English name or a pseudonym on their site.
  • 不放正脸照片，不写学校全名。 也不允许发布任何他人的个人信息。No face photos, no full school name — and no one else’s personal information, either.
  • 网站默认”不被搜索引擎收录”。 建站的那一周，我们会一步一步教孩子把这个开关关上：网站能通过链接打开，但搜不到。The site is set to no-index by default. In the week they build it, we walk them through switching that off step by step: the site opens by link, but does not appear in search results.
  • 要不要公开发布，是第二学年末才出现的一个选项，并且必须有家长的书面同意。 在那之前，不存在”公开”这个动作。Making the site public is only an option at the very end of the second semester, and only with written parental consent. Until then, going public is simply not an action that exists.
  • 所有工具都免费，注册只需一个邮箱——不要手机号，不需要 VPN。 这是这门课选工具的硬规则，不是偏好。All tools are free and sign up with an email address only — no phone number, no VPN. This is a hard rule for tool selection in this course, not a preference.
  • AI 的使用有明确规范。 第一学期不强制使用任何 AI 工具；部分工具的使用条款要求 14 岁以下的孩子获得监护人同意；课程规定：不得把 AI 生成的内容当作自己的作业提交，事实性内容必须另找一个可靠来源核实，任何时候都不得向 AI 工具输入自己或他人的个人信息。AI use has explicit rules. Nothing in Semester 1 requires an AI tool; some tools’ terms require guardian consent under 14; and the course rules are firm: AI-generated content may never be submitted as one’s own, factual claims must be verified against a reliable source, and personal information — your child’s or anyone else’s — is never to be typed into an AI tool.
  • 课程页面上的互动（打勾、自测、进度条）不需要任何账号，进度只保存在孩子自己的浏览器里，我们不收集。The interactive parts of each page — checkboxes, quizzes, progress bar — require no account at all; progress is stored only in your child’s own browser and is not collected by us.

如果网站出了问题、内容丢失、或孩子在网上遇到任何让他不舒服的事，请立刻给我发邮件。在这类事情上，报告得越早越好，从来没有”小题大做”这回事。

If the site breaks, content is lost, or your child meets anything online that makes them uncomfortable, please email me immediately. On matters like these, earlier is always better, and there is no such thing as overreacting.

六、家长可以怎样帮忙

6. How you can help

这一节是这封信真正的目的。下面每一条都是可以在这一周就做到的具体动作，不是口号。

This section is what the letter is for. Every item below is something you could do this week; none of them is a slogan.

1) 守住一个安静的两小时

1) Protect a quiet two hours

请和孩子一起，在一周里挑出两个约一小时的固定时段（例如周三晚上、周六上午），写进家里的日历，把它当作一堂真实的课。在这两个时段里：不派家务、不安排走亲访友、不在旁边开电视。异步课程最容易被”随时都可以做”这句话吃掉——“随时”最后往往变成”没有时候”。 时间被排进日历的孩子，和”想起来再说”的孩子，一学期下来差别非常大。

Together with your child, pick two roughly one-hour slots in the week (Wednesday evening and Saturday morning, say), write them into the family calendar, and treat them as a real class. During those two hours: no errands, no visits, no television in the background. Asynchronous courses are most often eaten by the phrase “you can do it anytime” — and “anytime” tends to become “no time.” Over a semester, the difference between a child whose hours are on the calendar and one who works “when they remember” is very large.

2) 问过程，不查答案

2) Ask about the process; do not audit the answers

孩子每周都会写三行日志：学到了什么 · 哪里卡住了 · 下周怎么办。 这三行是您和他聊学习最好的入口。可以这样问：

Every week your child writes three lines: what I learned · where I got stuck · what I’ll do next. Those three lines are the best possible opening for a conversation about learning. Try questions like:

  • “这周哪里卡住了？后来是怎么绕过去的？” — “Where did you get stuck this week, and how did you get around it?”
  • “这一条你打算下周怎么改？” — “What are you going to change about that next week?”
  • “这周你做的东西里，你自己最满意哪一处？” — “Which part of what you made this week are you happiest with?”

请不要问：“做完了吗？”“得了几分？”“老师说你哪里错了？”这三句话会把学习变成检查，而检查会让孩子学会隐藏，而不是学会求助。我在过去的家长讲座里说过一句话，今天仍然作数：交流不是家长提问、孩子回答，而是双方各说各的理解——有的聊，就是好的交流。

Please avoid: “Are you done?” “What did you score?” “What did the teacher say you got wrong?” Those three questions turn learning into inspection — and inspection teaches children to hide, not to ask for help. Something I have said in earlier parent talks still holds: a conversation is not a parent asking and a child answering; it is two people each saying how they see it. If there is something to talk about, that is already a good conversation.

3) 孩子卡住时，走这三步

3) When your child is stuck, follow these three steps

这门课有意把”卡住”当作学习的一部分，也教了孩子怎样求助。您能做的是陪他走流程，而不是替他解决：

This course treats being stuck as part of learning, and teaches children how to ask for help. What you can do is walk them through the process, not solve it for them:

  1. 让他把问题说清楚——一句话说明”我想做什么、我做了什么、结果怎样”，配一张截图。光是把问题写清楚，就能解决相当一部分问题。Have them state the problem — one sentence covering “what I was trying to do, what I did, what happened”, plus a screenshot. Simply writing the problem down clearly solves a fair share of problems outright.
  2. 让他自己给我发邮件。 不是您代发，也不是您代写——写邮件本身就是这门课第一学期的正式内容之一。我承诺在 24–48 小时内回复（节假日除外）。Have them email me themselves. Not you sending it, not you drafting it — writing that email is itself part of what Semester 1 teaches. I reply within 24–48 hours (holidays excepted).
  3. 等回复的时候，让他先做下一件事。 卡住的那一件放着，不是失败，是排队。While waiting, let them move to the next thing. A stuck item set aside is not a failure; it is an item in a queue.

只有一种情况请您直接出手：涉及安全、隐私、金钱或让孩子感到不安的事——那时请立刻联系我，不必等孩子自己开口。

There is one case where you should step in directly: anything involving safety, privacy, money, or something that has upset your child. Contact me at once; do not wait for your child to raise it.

4) 忍住替他做的冲动

4) Resist doing the work for them

这大概是最难的一条。孩子的录音会磕巴，短文会写得干瘪，网站会做得很朴素——而您五分钟就能替他改好。请不要。

This is probably the hardest one. Their audio recording will stumble, their short essay will read thin, their website will look plain — and you could fix all of it in five minutes. Please don’t.

一件由孩子自己做出来的、有瑕疵的作品，其价值远高于一件替他做好的完美作品，因为量规评的是”证据”：证据说明的是谁在思考。而且这门课几乎所有任务的评分依据都是”你做了什么、为什么这样做”，不是”做得多漂亮”。

A flawed piece your child made themselves is worth far more than a polished piece made for them, because the rubric reads evidence — and evidence shows who did the thinking. Nearly every task here is scored on “what you did and why”, not on how handsome the result looks.

您完全可以做的是：坐在旁边陪着；当他的第一位观众和听众；当他的”提问对象”（“你刚才说的那一步，我没听懂，能再讲一遍吗？”——被讲明白一次，胜过自己看三遍）。

What you can absolutely do: sit beside them; be their first audience; be the person they explain it to (“I didn’t follow that step — can you say it again?” — explaining something once beats re-reading it three times).

5) 表扬过程，而不是分数

5) Celebrate the process, not the score

请把夸奖放在这些地方：“你这次换了个方法”“你把卡住的地方写下来了”“你自己给导师写了邮件”“你这周按时坐下来了”。这些都是这门课真正想培养的东西。分数会来，也会走；能自己坐下来、自己开口求助、自己调整方法的孩子，走得最远。

Put your praise here: “You tried a different method this time.” “You wrote down where you got stuck.” “You emailed your mentor yourself.” “You sat down on time this week.” These are what the course is actually trying to grow. Scores come and go; the child who can sit down, ask for help, and adjust their own method goes the furthest.

6) 以身作则，并且允许自己示弱

6) Model it — and allow yourself to be the learner

如果不希望孩子在餐桌上看手机，最有效的一步是先把自己的手机从餐桌上拿走。反过来，也请偶尔让孩子教您一件他新学会的数字技能——怎么给截图打标注、怎么把一个网页保存下来。这件事有双重好处：他复习了，而您们之间多了一点互相信任的资本。在数码世界里，成年人并不处处强过孩子，承认这一点本身就是很好的家庭教育。

If you would rather your child not use a phone at the dinner table, the most effective first move is to take your own phone off it. Conversely, now and then let your child teach you something they have just learned — how to annotate a screenshot, how to save a web page. This does two things at once: they revise, and the two of you build a little more mutual trust. In the digital world adults are not stronger than children at every point, and admitting that is itself good parenting.

7) 留意反常，早说早好

7) Watch for the unusual, and speak early

如果您注意到孩子出现明显的反常——一向开朗的孩子突然沉默；一向舍不得关电脑的孩子连续几个周末碰都不碰；一向愿意把手机放在客厅的孩子忽然断然拒绝——请不要急着下结论，也不要急着责备，先温和地问，然后尽快联系我。这几种反常，在我过去的经验里，往往指向网络上遇到的具体麻烦。信息不充足的时候贸然下结论，可能会加深孩子的伤口。

If you notice a marked change — a cheerful child who suddenly goes quiet; a child who could never be pulled off the computer who now does not touch it for weekends on end; a child who always left their phone in the living room who now flatly refuses — do not rush to conclusions and do not rush to blame. Ask gently first, then contact me promptly. In my experience such changes often point to something specific that happened online. Concluding too quickly on too little information can deepen the wound.

8) 现在就可以开始聊”家庭媒体公约”

8) You can start the “family media agreement” conversation now

第二学期有一个单元，会请孩子起草一份家庭媒体公约，和家人一起商定，执行一周后再回来反思。您现在就可以先聊起来，几个起步的问题：

One Semester 2 module asks your child to draft a family media agreement, agree it with the family, live by it for a week, and then reflect. You can start that conversation now. A few opening questions:

  • 我们全家每天大概花多少时间在屏幕上？用得久了，我们各自是什么感觉？How much screen time does each of us have in a day — and how does a long stretch of it leave us feeling?
  • 什么内容适合发到网上，什么不适合？发照片之前应该检查什么？What is appropriate to post, and what is not? What should we check before posting a photo?
  • 看到不可靠的信息时，我们家怎么处理？When we see something online that looks unreliable, what do we do about it?
  • 如果有人在网上让我们感到不舒服，我们向谁说？If someone online makes us uncomfortable, whom do we tell?

三条经验之谈：用正面的措辞写（写”我们会……“而不是一长串”不许……“）；公约要适用于每一位家庭成员，包括家长；贴在冰箱上，并且约好多久回顾一次。

Three practical notes: write it in the positive (“we will…”, rather than a long list of “don’ts”); make it apply to every family member, parents included; and put it on the fridge, with an agreed date to revisit it.
